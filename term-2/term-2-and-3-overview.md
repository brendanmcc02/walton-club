# Term 2 & 3 Session Overview

In the first class, I want to tell the students about their presentations for class number 9 and what's expected of them, just so they are aware of the plan for the next few months. Rather than just giving them 2-3 full, unstructured classes to work on their project, I plan to guide them step by step through the entire process.

For instance, in the first class I'll talk about what a research question is, and why it's useful, and then give some examples. At the end of the class, I'll ask the class to think of a cool research question for an area they're interested in (e.g. movies, airplanes, lego, etc.)

In the next class, we can get our hands on working with datasets, that could involve filtering out different columns, filtering out rows based on some criteria (e.g. movies released after 2015). At the end of the class, I'll ask the students to pick a dataset that is relevant to their research question and interest, and to load in the dataset itself. etc. etc.

This means the project gets done incrementally and not in a rushed way, meaning they have guided support along the way.

## Lesson 1 - intro to data science

### Lecture
* what is a dataset, .csv file, etc.
* what kinds of things can you do with data science?
* explain the poster idea for term and show them examples of posters from last year

### Exercise
* get them to think about something they enjoy (e.g. movies, sports) and to pick an area of interest
    * i love books, so i'll use that as an example
* explain what a research question is
    * why is it done
    * and give a few examples, e.g: 
        * "do longer books have higher ratings?"
        * "are most football goals scored inside the box?"

## Lesson 2 - Pandas

*The entire lesson will be hands-on/interactive as opposed to lecture-style.*

### Exercise

* how to load in a .csv file
* print the dataset to the console and get a quick look at it
* get the students to load their dataset in
* give them a sample .csv file
* get them to filter a dataset by a specific column value
    * e.g. only pick movies released after the year 2015
* **advanced:** get them to group different rows together based on a column value, e.g. group all 'comedy' movies together and get the average rating
* get the students to find a dataset relevant to their research question and area of interest on Kaggle

## Lesson 3 - Visualising Data (Part 1)

### Lecture
* explain the importance of visualising data
* the unique value of different charts, e.g. bar charts, pie charts, histograms, etc.

### Exercise
* give them some sample csv files
* ask them to group different rows together based on a column value (e.g. movies that are 'comedy')
* display this on a bar chart

## Lesson 4 - Visualising Data (Part 2)

No lecture for this lesson. I want to reinforce concepts from last week and ensure all the work is "hands-on".

### Exercise
* give them sample csv files
* ask them to group the different rows together based on a column value (e.g. 30-minute intervals for movie runtime - <60m, <90m, etc.)
* ask them to do a scatterplot: plot an independent variable (e.g. movie runtime), and then a dependent variable (e.g. movie ratings)
* ask them if they think there is a correlation between the variables
