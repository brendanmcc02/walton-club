# Notes

## What is neurodiversity

* 80-85% of the population is "neurotypical"
    * your brain developed in the "typical" way
    * called typical because most people develop their brain in that way
* opposite of this obviously is "neurodivergent"
* a "neurotype" is a neurological type, and autism is a neurotype

## What is autism

* a lifelong developmental disability/difference in how individuals communicate/interact with others and experience the world around them

* Identity first language - "autistic people"
* Person first language - "people with autism"
* generally there is a preference towards identity-first language, so use that as a default - their advice
    * but it does depend on the person
    * some might prefer identity-first, others may prefer person-first - ask them!
* I found this very surprising and unexpected, I would have guessed people prefer person-first because the autism is less definitive
    * I asked the trainer about this
    * she said that it's slowly no longer being seen as a taboo and the awareness around autism is growing 
    * so people wear it with pride like a badge of honour
    * so most autistic people are generally ok with identity first language

* **masking** coping mechanism that autistic people use to mask their "different" traits in order to fit in
    * really harmful for their long-term development
    * want to encourage people to authentically be themselves

## Strengths of neurodivergence
* hyperfocus and creativity
* attention to detail, observational skills, strong memory
* strong sense of justice and right/wrong

* **hyposensitive** - no/lack of sensitivity to stimulus
* **hypersensitive** - enhanced sensitivity to stimulus

*"Autistic people must learn scientifically [empirically] what non autistic people learn instinctively"*

* autistic people generally have a preference for deep conversations about interests and spending long time on one topic
    * many struggle with small talk or will avoid it entirely
* 20% of autistic people are non-speaking

## Stimming
* action of repetitive behaviour to help emotional regulation
    * e.g. tapping
    * making noise
    * hand flapping, hand movements
* very helpful for autistic people to let them stim and not to stop them

## Predictability
* many autistic people have a preference for routine and predictability
* they feel much safer when a consistent routine is in place
* often helpful to let people know things in advance about the venue
    * e.g. toilets, parking situation
    * makes them feel more comfortable coming into the physical space, greater awareness of surroundings
* many autistic people are unaware of unwritten social contracts and may take time to learn them or be unaware
    * for example, telling them "you can go to the toilet" is helpful, i.e. just setting expectations rather than letting them assume expectations which many autistic people generally struggle with
* she said that writing down rules is very helpful, makes it more concrete and less like an unwritten rule
    * "if it's not written down, then it is not a rule"
